\section{Robustness and Confounds}
\label{sec:robustness}
% =====================================================================

A templated benchmark and a within-model ablation invite specific confounds---option
artefacts, letter skew, image-count asymmetry, shared label priors. We check each, and
the headline survives all of them for one structural reason we make precise first: it
is a within-item comparison.

\subsubsection{The headline is a within-item difference, so shared artefacts cancel.}
The central result---that \textsf{full} loses to the per-item single-view oracle by
\num{12.3} points---compares two predictions on the \emph{same} question, with
byte-identical text and options, differing only in which images are supplied. Any
effect that does not depend on the image condition---a label prior, an option-length
regularity, a template the model has memorised, a residual text shortcut---contributes
equally to both sides and subtracts out of the difference. The interference we measure
therefore cannot be manufactured by text leakage: a model that read the answer off the
text alone would score identically under \textsf{sat}, \textsf{sv}, and \textsf{full},
yielding zero synergy and zero oracle gap, not the systematic loss we observe---and
this holds regardless of how clean the absolute accuracies are. Empirically the model
also reads the imagery (\cref{sec:main}): each single view scores far above the
\num{25.2}\% chance rate, so the comparison is between two genuinely vision-driven
predictions. No model refused or
hedged on any urban item.

\subsubsection{Option text and position.}
Even so, we verify the answer cannot be read off the templated options. On the seven
urban-attribute tasks the correct letter is uniform (A/B/C/D $=$
\num{662}/\num{694}/\num{628}/\num{645}; $\chi^2=\num{3.6}$, $p=\num{0.31}$ against
uniform; an always-`A' baseline scores \num{25.2}\%), and the correct option is not
systematically the longest---its mean length (\num{25.9} characters) is slightly below
that of the distractors (\num{26.5}), and it is the single longest option on only
\num{22.9}\% of items, below the \num{25}\% chance rate. Across the full
\num{12}-task benchmark the letter distribution is skewed (always-`A' \num{29.3}\%),
an artefact of the option-shuffling RNG; that skew is carried entirely by the
cross-view tasks (\num{34.4}\%) and is absent from the urban tasks evaluated here.

\subsubsection{Image count.}
A concern in multi-view ablations is that one condition may simply present more
images than another. In our primary configuration this concern does not arise: the
\textsf{sat}, \textsf{sv}, and \textsf{full} conditions supply one, one, and two
images respectively, so the comparison of \textsf{full} against either single view
adds exactly one image of the other modality, with no token-count asymmetry between
the two views. (The cross-view control tasks of \cref{sec:control} feed the full
street-view set by construction; we treat them as a sensitivity check on the
instrument, not as an image-count-matched comparison, and the headline result does
not depend on them.)

\subsubsection{OSM provenance.}
Our labels are OSM-derived, and two of the four zero-shot remote-sensing models we
compare against---LHRS-Bot-Nova and EarthDial---are trained on OSM-derived text, so
their scores may reflect shared OSM priors; we treat them as an upper bound on
visual skill and mark them accordingly (\cref{sec:zeroshot}). This does not touch
our main result, which is a \emph{within-model} difference (\textsf{full} vs.\ the
better single view): any view-independent OSM prior cancels in the difference.

\subsubsection{Satellite resolution.}
The redundancy is measured against high-resolution overhead imagery, not a coarse
proxy: every benchmark tile resolves to a sub-meter source (ESRI
$\sim$\SIrange{0.3}{0.5}{m/px}, NAIP $\sim$\SI{0.6}{m/px}, IGN $\sim$\SI{0.2}{m/px}).
For tasks that are structurally ground-level no overhead resolution recovers the cue. We cannot rule out that still
higher ground sampling distance would help the few tasks where overhead geometry is
plausibly resolution-limited (building height, urban density), and flag this as the
main open question (\cref{sec:limitations}).
