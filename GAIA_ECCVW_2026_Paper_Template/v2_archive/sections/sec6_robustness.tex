% =====================================================================
\section{Robustness and Confounds}
\label{sec:robustness}
% =====================================================================

We check the confounds a templated benchmark and a within-model ablation invite.

\subsubsection{Option text and position.}
Because the questions are templated, we verify the answer cannot be read off the
options. On the seven urban-attribute tasks that carry our headline result, the
correct letter is uniform (A/B/C/D $=$ \num{662}/\num{694}/\num{628}/\num{645};
$\chi^2=\num{3.6}$, $p=\num{0.31}$ against uniform; an always-`A' baseline scores
\num{25.2}\%), and the correct option is not systematically the longest---its mean
length (\num{25.9} characters) is slightly below that of the distractors
(\num{26.5}), and it is the single longest option on only \num{22.9}\% of items,
below the \num{25}\% chance rate. We note for completeness that across the full
\num{12}-task corpus the letter distribution is skewed (always-`A' \num{29.3}\%),
an artefact of the option-shuffling RNG documented in the dataset report; this skew
is carried by the cross-view tasks (always-`A' \num{34.4}\%) and is absent from the
urban tasks evaluated here. Crucially, our headline metric is a within-item
comparison across image conditions (\textsf{full} vs.\ a single view) on identical
option text, so any residual option regularity cancels in the difference and cannot
produce the effect.

\subsubsection{Text-only leakage and degenerate tasks.}
We treat $\textsf{full}-\textsf{blind}$ as the vision-attributable signal and audit
every task in \textsf{blind} mode. This audit removed two tasks from the released
benchmark: \textsf{green\_space}, which a model answered at \num{100}\% accuracy with
no image (the correct option wording was identifiable independent of its position),
and \textsf{road\_surface}, which was near-ceiling and essentially text-solvable.
Removing degenerate tasks before reporting is a strength, not a gap: it leaves the
seven urban tasks, on each of which the imagery contributes a meaningful margin over
the text-only prior (for example $+\num{22}$ points of vision contribution on
\textsf{transit\_density} and $+\num{29}$ on \textsf{amenity\_richness};
\cref{sec:main}). The interference result of \cref{sec:itemlevel} is, moreover,
robust to any residual leakage: the per-item single-view oracle beats the two-view
model by \num{12.3} points overall and by \num{12.2} points when restricted to the
\num{1357} items the model answers \emph{incorrectly} while blind---the items that
genuinely require vision. No model refused or hedged on any urban item in any mode.

\subsubsection{Image count.}
A concern in multi-view ablations is that one condition may simply present more
images than another. In our primary configuration this concern does not arise: the
\textsf{sat}, \textsf{sv}, and \textsf{full} conditions supply one, one, and two
images respectively, so the comparison of \textsf{full} against either single view
adds exactly one image of the other modality, with no token-count asymmetry between
the two views. (The cross-view control tasks of \cref{sec:control} feed the full
street-view set by construction; we treat them as a sensitivity check on the
instrument, not as an image-count-matched comparison, and the headline result does
not depend on them.)

\subsubsection{OSM provenance.}
Our labels are OSM-derived, and two of the four zero-shot remote-sensing models we
compare against---LHRS-Bot-Nova and EarthDial---are trained on OSM-derived text, so
their scores may reflect shared OSM priors; we treat them as an upper bound on
visual skill and mark them accordingly (\cref{sec:zeroshot}). This does not touch
our main result, which is a \emph{within-model} difference (\textsf{full} vs.\ the
better single view): any view-independent OSM prior cancels in the difference.

\subsubsection{Satellite resolution.}
The redundancy is measured against high-resolution overhead imagery, not a coarse
proxy: every benchmark tile resolves to a sub-meter source (ESRI
$\sim$\SIrange{0.3}{0.5}{m/px}, NAIP $\sim$\SI{0.6}{m/px}, IGN $\sim$\SI{0.2}{m/px}),
and the Sentinel-2 fallback is never triggered. For tasks that are structurally
ground-level no overhead resolution recovers the cue. We cannot rule out that still
higher ground sampling distance would help the few tasks where overhead geometry is
plausibly resolution-limited (building height, urban density), and flag this as the
main open question (\cref{sec:limitations}).
