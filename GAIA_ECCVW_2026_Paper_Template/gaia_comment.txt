# Final Revision Instructions for ECCV Workshop Submission

## Overall Objective

The paper is scientifically mature and the experimental story is complete. This revision should **not introduce new experiments or substantially change the scientific conclusions**. Instead, the goal is to maximize reviewer readability while reducing the manuscript from approximately **15 pages to 13–14 pages** (preferably around **13.5 pages before references**), leaving room for a few additional figures.

This should be treated as a **presentation and writing optimization pass**, not a research revision.

---

# Primary Goals (Highest Priority)

1. Reduce the manuscript to approximately 13–14 pages.
2. Improve visual presentation by adding 2–3 high-impact figures.
3. Reduce redundancy throughout the paper.
4. Slightly soften causal/mechanistic claims.
5. Make the paper read more naturally and less over-edited.
6. Improve reviewer experience by making the central message immediately obvious.

No new experiments are required unless they are trivial to produce.

---

# 1. Preserve the Existing Scientific Story

Do **not** substantially change the paper's narrative.

The paper should continue to follow this progression:

Existing benchmarks assume multiple views help

↓

OELLM measures whether they actually help

↓

Urban semantic tasks show almost no measurable benefit

↓

Cross-view tasks show very large benefits

↓

Therefore, multi-view usefulness is fundamentally task-dependent.

This narrative is one of the strongest aspects of the current manuscript and should remain intact.

---

# 2. Reduce Length (Highest Editing Priority)

Current manuscript is longer than necessary.

Target:

Reduce approximately 1–2 pages while improving readability.

Focus on removing repeated explanations rather than deleting technical content.

Specifically:

* Remove repeated descriptions of benchmark motivation.

* Remove repeated explanations that urban tasks test whether the second modality adds value.

* Compress repeated reminders of limitations.

* Compress repeated summaries at the end of sections.

* Merge consecutive paragraphs that make essentially the same point.

The reader should only need to hear each major idea once.

---

# 3. Remove Writing Redundancy

Several ideas appear multiple times.

Reduce repetition of statements such as:

* current VLMs derive little benefit from the second view

* urban tasks differ from cross-view tasks

* the benchmark measures complementarity

* the paper does not overclaim

Once introduced clearly, later sections should simply reference the earlier explanation rather than re-explaining it.

---

# 4. Reduce Meta-Writing

The manuscript currently contains many sentences describing what the paper is doing rather than simply presenting the evidence.

Reduce phrases such as:

* We are careful...

* We emphasize...

* We note...

* We stress...

* We do not claim...

* Two facts stand out...

* This establishes...

Instead, state the evidence directly.

For example:

Instead of

"We are careful not to overclaim."

Prefer

"The experiments do not distinguish among these explanations."

Readers naturally infer the paper is being careful.

---

# 5. Slightly Humanize the Writing

The writing is technically excellent but somewhat uniformly polished.

The goal is NOT to make it casual.

Instead, make it sound more like an experienced researcher.

Specifically:

* Vary sentence length.

* Allow some paragraphs to end immediately after presenting evidence.

* Avoid ending every paragraph with an interpretive summary.

* Reduce overly polished transitions.

* Accept slightly more direct wording.

The paper should feel less like every paragraph has been optimized independently.

---

# 6. Keep Claims Strictly Behavioral

This is the most important scientific wording change.

The experiments demonstrate:

* adding the second view rarely improves accuracy

* the better single view usually performs as well as both views

* cross-view tasks are different

The experiments do NOT identify why this happens internally.

Avoid implying mechanisms.

Avoid statements like:

* the model selects one modality

* the model is distracted

* fusion fails

Prefer:

* the model derives little measurable benefit

* the second view rarely improves prediction

* combined input seldom exceeds the better single view

* the experiments do not distinguish among possible mechanisms

Maintain the cautious tone already used in the Discussion section throughout the paper.

---

# 7. Strengthen Reviewer Defenses

One likely reviewer criticism is:

"The benchmark was designed so that one modality is sufficient."

Address this proactively.

Clarify that:

The contribution is NOT demonstrating that one modality can solve the task.

Rather, it demonstrates that even when two informative modalities are available, current VLMs derive almost no measurable improvement from combining them.

The contribution concerns utilization of redundant multimodal information.

---

# 8. Surface the Strongest Result Earlier

The strongest empirical finding is not the average synergy.

It is the item-level audit.

Specifically:

Fusion rescues very few predictions while overturning many correct ones.

Mention this result earlier.

Ideally:

* Abstract

* End of Introduction

* Beginning of Results

This statistic is memorable and easy for reviewers to recall.
