\begin{frame}{The goal}
\large
Every question feeds the model \textbf{4 street-view images} of the same spot:
the \textbf{forward view}, the back view, and the two side views.
\vspace{8pt}
\begin{block}{What we want to know}
Can we \textbf{throw most of them away}? If one image already gives the right
answer, feeding four is wasted compute.
\end{block}
\vspace{8pt}
\footnotesize
\textbf{How we test it.} Solve each question on all 4 images, see which image the
model \emph{looks at} most, then keep only the top $k$ images and ask again.
\par\vspace{3pt}
\textbf{Survival \%} $=$ of the questions it got right on 4 images, how many it
\emph{still} gets right on the smaller set.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Does the \emph{choice} of image matter?}
\large
\begin{itemize}
\item A random single image already survives \textbf{85\%}. So a lot of the answer
 isn't really about \emph{which} image you keep.
\item But the image the model \textbf{looks at} does a bit better:
 \textbf{89.5\%} vs \textbf{85\%} --- a real \textbf{$+4.4$ pt} edge over a random pick.
\item And the image it \textbf{ignores} is worst (83\%) --- so the model's attention
 is pointing at something useful, not noise.
\end{itemize}
\vspace{6pt}
\footnotesize
On the harder questions --- the ones that genuinely need to look at an image ---
the edge of the attended image over a random one grows to \textbf{$+8.8$ pt}.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Take-home}
\large
\begin{itemize}
\item[\textcolor{good}{\ding{51}}] We \textbf{don't need all 4 street-view images} ---
 \textbf{one} is enough ($89.5\%$ of answers survive).
\item[\textcolor{good}{\ding{51}}] That one image is the \textbf{forward view}: it's
 what the model looks at $94.8\%$ of the time, so just always keep it.
\item[\textcolor{good}{\ding{51}}] The looked-at image beats a random one by
 \textbf{$+4.4$ pt} overall (\textbf{$+8.8$ pt} on the questions that really need an
 image) --- attention is pointing at something real, in every topic.
\end{itemize}
\vspace{6pt}
